# HOW TO DECIDE, IN THIS ORDER

1. DELIVERY. Is every {{deliverable}} named by the instructions present, at the requested path, in the requested form, with every named part?

2. CONFORMANCE. Is it ONLY what was asked for? Where the instructions name files, fields, sections or an order, those define the artifact's SHAPE, and the shape is part of the request. Extra files, added commentary, renamed or re-worded identifiers, reordered fields and unrelated rewrites are DEVIATIONS, not improvements -- however helpful they look. A downstream reader may key on exact names and positions, and cannot ask you what you meant. Where the instruction is explicit, match it exactly; where it is silent, prefer the plainest form that satisfies it.
   A value the request asks for must be PRESENT AS A VALUE. An artifact that leaves the reader to finish the work -- a placeholder to be filled, a described-but-not-performed change -- has not delivered it.

3. FIDELITY. CHECK THE CLAIMS AGAINST `context/source/`, which holds the material the work was done from. Open it and verify the facts, names, figures and constraints a candidate asserts -- do not take its word for them, and do not settle for whether it sounds authoritative. Reject invented specifics. Where candidates disagree, the source decides it; where the source is silent, prefer the candidate that says so over the one that fills the gap confidently.

4. INTERNAL CONSISTENCY. Does the artifact agree with itself? Call sites that match the signatures they call, tests that exercise what the change claims, cross-references that resolve, counts that match the number of items actually present. A part that contradicts another part of the same artifact is a defect even when you cannot tell which one is wrong.

5. OPERATIONAL FITNESS. Is this the right kind of artifact, and can the intended person or system actually use, run, build or act on it without reconstructing missing work? Judge this as the requester's downstream consumer, not as a reviewer wanting to be convinced -- an addition that helps a human read the change but breaks the build is a cost, not a benefit.

6. FINISH. No placeholders, TODOs, unresolved notes, broken references, empty required parts, visibly incomplete sections, or content that does not match its declared format. A renderer-truncation marker inserted for this comparison is not a defect in the original artifact; judge only what is actually visible.

Length and polish are not merits in themselves. A shorter, correct, complete, exactly-shaped answer beats a longer one with a gap or an embellishment.
